ALIENI

Business Platform Model
& Development Plan

Working document for discussion and decision-making

Purpose: to organise the business-side services, customer journeys,
payment options and development phases before any changes are built or
payment systems are activated. This is a planning document, not a final
commercial or legal decision.

1. The main decision Alieni needs to make

Before building the business payment system, Alieni needs to decide what
role it will play in each service. This affects who collects money, who
pays the cleaner, who handles complaints and cancellations, and what
responsibility Alieni takes for the cleaning service.

Marketplace / connection platform

Alieni introduces a business to an independent cleaner. The business and
cleaner agree the work and payment arrangement. Alieni earns through a
platform fee, commission or another clearly explained charge.

Managed cleaning service

The business books through Alieni. Alieni arranges the cleaner and
manages delivery of the service. The business pays Alieni, and Alieni
pays the cleaner. This gives Alieni more control but also creates more
operational responsibility.

Hybrid model

Alieni uses different models for different service categories. For
example, it may introduce cleaners for some jobs but manage bookings and
payments for other services.

Decision still required: choose the model Alieni wants to operate, or
decide whether a hybrid approach is appropriate. Do not assume that
every business customer should pay a monthly subscription.

2. Business customer types and service categories

The business side should not present every service as one mixed job or
booking area. A business should be able to enter the section that
matches its actual need and follow a flow designed for that service.

-   Moving-in cleaning

-   Moving-out / end-of-tenancy cleaning

-   Deep cleaning

-   Post-renovation cleaning

-   Spring cleaning

-   One-time commercial cleaning

B. Regular commercial cleaning contracts

-   Ongoing cleaning for offices

-   Schools

-   Shops

-   Clinics

-   Other commercial premises

C. Emergency and on-demand replacement cover

-   A hotel needs a replacement because a cleaner is absent

-   An Airbnb host's usual cleaner is unavailable

-   An office or other business needs temporary or last-minute cover

D. Hospitality and property turnovers

-   Airbnb turnovers

-   Hotel room cleaning

-   Holiday rentals

-   Short-stay apartments and other accommodation turnovers

E. Finding and connecting with cleaners

-   Businesses search cleaner profiles

-   Businesses connect with suitable cleaners

-   Businesses may post a job or request responses from cleaners

3. Pricing and payment options to compare

The pricing model should match how often the business uses Alieni and
the value Alieni provides. The household-side idea of a monthly fee
(approximately €15, still to be confirmed) does not automatically mean
that every business should pay a monthly fee.

Monthly subscription

A business pays regularly for ongoing access or a defined set of
services. This may suit businesses with frequent needs, but the benefits
must be clear and worthwhile.

Pay per booking / pay per job

A business pays when it actually books a one-off clean or replacement
cleaner. This may be more suitable for occasional users who do not need
a monthly membership.

Per-turnover fee

A business pays for each completed property or room turnover. This could
suit Airbnb and short-stay accommodation operators whose needs vary with
bookings.

Regular contract arrangement

A recurring commercial cleaning customer agrees a schedule and price for
ongoing work. Billing may be weekly or monthly, depending on the agreed
arrangement.

Hybrid pricing

Different categories use different payment methods. For example, one-off
cleaning could be pay-per-job, while regular contract customers use
recurring billing.

4. Specific question: emergency cover for hotels and other businesses

A hotel may already employ its own cleaners and only need a replacement
when someone is absent. It may not make sense to charge that hotel a
monthly subscription simply to access emergency cover if it only uses
the service occasionally.

The following options should be compared:

-   Pay-as-needed: the business pays only when it books a replacement
    cleaner.

-   Optional priority-access membership: a business pays a membership
    fee only if Alieni can genuinely provide a defined priority service
    or access to a reserved pool of cleaners. A separate booking charge
    may still apply.

-   Regular cover contract: a business pays for an agreed level of
    recurring or guaranteed cover, with the availability and response
    terms clearly defined.

Important: Alieni should not promise guaranteed emergency availability
or priority access unless it has the cleaner supply and operating
process to deliver it.

7. Suggested development phases

Build the business side in manageable stages. The order below is a
starting proposal and can be changed after reviewing operational
complexity, demand and profitability.

Phase 1 — Business profiles and cleaner connections

Business registration and profiles; business search for cleaners;
cleaner profiles; job adverts or requests; responses, messaging and
connections.

Phase 2 — One-off cleaning bookings

Create the booking flow for moving-in, moving-out, deep,
post-renovation, spring and one-time commercial cleaning. Agree the
payment trigger and cancellation process before implementation.

Phase 3 — Regular commercial cleaning

Support recurring contracts, agreed schedules, ongoing cleaner
arrangements and recurring invoices or billing.

Phase 4 — Hospitality and property turnovers

Add Airbnb, hotel and short-stay turnover workflows, calendars,
schedules and repeat bookings.

Phase 5 — Advanced business management

Add more advanced reporting, operational controls, service performance
tracking and other management tools when the earlier phases are working
reliably.

8. Questions that must be answered before implementation

1.  Is Alieni a marketplace, a managed cleaning service, or a hybrid?
    Does the answer differ by service category?

2.  Who receives the customer's payment: the cleaner or Alieni?

3.  When is a one-off booking charged: at booking, after a cleaner
    accepts, or after the work is completed?

4.  How does Alieni earn money: subscription, commission, booking fee,
    per-turnover fee, contract margin, or a combination?

5.  Who pays the cleaner, and when?

6.  What happens if a cleaner cancels, does not arrive, or the customer
    is unhappy with the work?

7.  What cancellation, refund and dispute rules will apply?

8.  Can Alieni reliably provide emergency cover, and what response times
    can it honestly promise?

9.  Which business services should be included in the first launch, and
    which should wait for later phases?

9. Operational and commercial checks

Before committing to a model, assess whether Alieni can operate it
reliably and profitably. Consider cleaner availability, vetting and
onboarding, travel and scheduling, customer support, payment processing
costs, insurance, complaints, cancellations, refunds and the time
required to manage each booking. The legal and insurance implications
should be checked for the countries and service types in which Alieni
will operate.

10. Instructions for the development team

-   Review the existing Alieni codebase, database, roles and current
    business features before changing anything.

-   Do not rebuild the platform or replace working features
    unnecessarily.

-   Do not implement or activate new payment flows until the business
    model and customer journeys have been approved.

-   Keep business service categories distinct in the interface and
    database where appropriate.

-   Design each category with its own relevant customer journey rather
    than putting all services into one generic booking flow.

-   Ensure cleaner work preferences are used in job matching.

-   Keep role permissions and data access secure on the server side and
    through database access policies.

-   Present the proposed business structure, flows, payment options and
    phased build plan for approval before coding.

11. Current status
